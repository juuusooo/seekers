## 14 Nov 12:40 · open
- **Hypothesis:** a model (LLM) reading a large sample of dealer notes, checked against the reveal truth, finds note classes that kit-best's reader ignores or misreads (site hours, summer tyres, payment release, soft hints) and each class has a measurable cost per order that a price or plan rule can use
- **Base, seeds, metric:** recorded weeks in runs/events (reveal truth of won orders, no new weeks played); share of notes per truth class, reader hit rate per class, failed visits and late share per class on weeks played by kit-best-like configs; LLM labels on a stratified sample of unique notes against the truth
- **Kill criteria:** no class with both a share over 1% of orders and a reader miss or a cost: then the reader is done and notes stay as they are
- **What I did:** No weeks played. `build.py`: 2,301 recorded level 8 weeks of today (kit-max, kit-lab, kit-best; all with the note reader) into `runs/notes/orders.jsonl` (883,386 won orders, 375,850 with a note, 34,645 different texts), each with the reveal truth. `classes.py`: truth class, late share and failed visits per class. `reader_check.ts`: kit-best's `readNote` and the plates / manual words against the truth. Model pass: 1,496 different notes (stratified, reader misses over-weighted), read blind by 10 Sonnet readers (`runs/notes/llm/TASK.md`, `in_*.jsonl`, `out_*.jsonl`, truth in `key.jsonl`), scored by `score_llm.py`. `hours.py`: a prototype reader of site hours, scored on all notes and on 500 other weeks.
- **Result / verdict:** fact.
  - 42.5% of won orders have a note. Every note has at most ONE real effect; other sentences are decoys (stated hours repeated, a deadline equal to the option's, a ready time already in the window).
  - Of notes: harmless 70.4%, ready later 7.4%, pickup hours 5.3%, tighter deadline 5.2%, delivery hours 3.4%, payment 2.9%, manual 2.3%, plates 2.1%, summer tyres 1.0%.
  - Per week (389 orders): class, orders, late share, extra late orders against no note (4.0% late), failed visits:
    pickup hours 8.7, 14.1%, +0.88, 2.38 `closed` · delivery hours 5.5, 19.4%, +0.85, 0 · deadline 8.5, 10.2%, +0.53, 0 · plates 3.4, 9.7%, +0.20, 0.50 `no_plates` · ready later 12.0, 4.6%, +0.07, 0.17 · payment, manual, tyres: at the base.
  - kit-best's reader is right on 98.0% (ready), 98.1% (payment), 97.4% (deadline), 99.1% (manual), 99.3% (plates) of the notes of its classes, 99.9% no false alarm on harmless. It does not read site hours at all: that is where the cost is.
  - Delivery hours: 24% of these orders arrive after the real closing time and count at the next opening (1,844 of the 2,441 late ones). Real closing is 2 to 6 hours before the stated one (pickup 14:00 to 17:00, delivery 15:00 to 18:00); 29% also open 1 to 3 hours later.
  - Hours with no site named ("open 10-15", "we close at 14:30", "aukioloajat", "liike auki") always mean the PICKUP site. Delivery hours always name it (vastaanotto, kohde, perillä, toimitusosoite, receiving, destination, mottagaren).
  - Model (Sonnet, blind): right effect (main or second) 98 to 100% in every class with an effect, times right whenever the effect was the main one. Its "misses" on harmless (16%) are decoys it cannot know without the option deadline and the window. Soft tags it found (greeting, spare wheels, keys, reference, call first, urgent, customer collects, damage, documents, low fuel): none moves the late share (4.2 to 4.9% against 4.4%), so "harmless" needs no finer class. Trade-in notes are 14% late, but that is the two-car order, not the note.
  - `hours.py` (regex, no model in the loop): hours right on 99.69% of pickup-hours notes, 99.94% of delivery-hours notes, 99.95% of all notes; on 500 weeks not used: 99.62%, 99.90% (mostly the same texts: only 906 new texts, all right).
  - Side find (bug, not a note-reading fault): `tryPair` in `kit-best/core/engine.ts` posts the pair with `FIRST_TRY` and `manualLicence: false`, so the NEW order's plates / manual note is lost in a two-car gig. 140 of 150 `no_plates` and 33 of 34 `licence` failed visits were in two-car gigs; plates orders fail 14.5 per 100 although the words are found in 99.3%.

- **Hypothesis:** reading real site hours from notes (one-day stop windows that roll to the next day, price and fees count the last pickup inside real hours, such cars go alone) and keeping plates / manual orders out of two-car gigs raise margin and on-time share
- **Base, seeds, metric:** kit-notes (= kit-best, replay equal to the cent on m-1..4 with switches off); fresh seeds nt-1..32 same weeks: base, pairFlags=1, noteHours=1, both; then winner on fresh nh- seeds and on BEST3; mean difference ± SE, weeks better and worse, ranked weeks, worst on-time share
- **Kill criteria:** under +1 SE on nt- for both switches, or fewer ranked weeks
- **What I did:** `kit-notes/` = kit-best + three switches (default off, replay equal to the cent on m-1..4): `noteHours` (TS port of the hours reader in `core/notes.ts`, 99.55% / 99.94% right on the reveal; one-day stop windows that roll on `gig_expired`; `lastSlot` in quote and fees; such cars solo; urgent fees 3 h before the real last pickup), `hoursMinLeftH=1.5`, `pairFlags` (plates / manual order never joins a pair). Sweep `nt1` on fresh seeds nt-1..32, 4 variants; world data pulled with `pull_mine.ts`, per class with `by_class.py`.
  - Screen nt-1..32 (base 16,289, 31/32 ranked, worst 87.6%): pairFlags +58 ± 78 (17/12, 31/32, worst 87.0%); noteHours +3 ± 114 (18/14, 31/32, worst 87.1%); both -11 ± 126 (16/16, 31/32, worst 88.9%). Mean on-time 95.2% → 95.5% (nh, both).
  - Mechanism (per week, base → both): failed visits 3.53 → 0.56; pickup-hours orders late 11.8% → 4.1%, failed visits 2.50 → 0.16; delivery-hours orders late 19.9% → 1.9%; plates orders late 12.4% → 5.8%, failed visits 0.62 → 0.06; all late orders 17.7 → 16.6.
