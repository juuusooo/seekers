Seekers: the Bahn Spread system that played the final (BEST16, level 8)
=====================================================================

Final: 19,975 margin, 95.4% on time, 432 orders. On 96 fresh practice weeks it makes 18,738 a week
(95 of 96 ranked), +515 ± 80 over the previous version on the same weeks.

The rules below were first written for BEST4. Every value in this file is the BEST16 value. Rules
added after BEST4 are listed in "Added after BEST4" at the end, with their parameters.

One idea drives everything: for every car, the booking model estimates the fee at which a driver
takes it in time. That number decides which requests to take, what price to quote and what fee to
pay.

HOW TO RUN
----------
cd Bahn-Spread/kit-best
set -a && source ../.env && set +a          # loads the team keys (never print .env)
bun core/run.ts --seed my-test-1             # one practice week, level 8
bun core/run.ts --seed my-test-1 --set capLam=5   # the same week with one value changed
bash core/tools/sweep.sh 48 m mytest 'base|' 'idea|capLam=5'   # test an idea on 48 seeds
python3 core/tools/cmp.py mytest base        # difference ± SE, weeks better/worse

HOW IT DECIDES
--------------
1. Request arrives
   Replace the dealer's typical fee by the lane-table prediction (rule 1). Read the note: ready
   later, must arrive by (rules 2, 3).

2. Options
   For each delivery option: plan the cheapest fee schedule with the booking model. That gives the
   expected fee and the chance a driver books in time (rule 6). Drop options under 85% (rule 7);
   sell 85 to 95% options 0.2 x list dearer (rule 8).

3. Capacity
   A full pickup town or a full deadline day is priced 0.25 x list higher, not declined (rules 9,
   10).

4. Price
   Search 1.28 to 2.0 x list for the price with the highest chance to win x (price - expected fee -
   4 EUR per driver-hour). Shift -0.05 for a round trip, +0.3 in a competitor outage (rules 11 to
   14). Decline with 10 x list when nothing is left (rule 16).

5. Won
   Join an open one-car gig nearby as a two-car gig (split after 4 h unbooked), or post alone
   (rules 24 to 26). Premium, plates, manual gearbox and charging handled at posting (rules 27 to
   29).

6. Fees
   Planned opening fee with hourly raises, early ramp to 1.6 x typical in the last 10 h, deadline
   warning: 1.3 x typical +0.15 every 30 min, past the last pickup time: back to 1.2 (rules 19 to
   23).

7. Learn
   After every answer, refit this week's competitor price level on the last 300 answers (rule 17).
   Dealer trust counts on-time orders (rule 18).

RULES (all 35, in the order a request meets them)
-------------------------------------------------
 1. Denoise the typical fee (request) [typMinN = 5]
    Replaces the dealer's typical fee by the lane-table prediction (typ.json: lane + private
    address + premium + electric; trade-in = 0.50 x sum of legs). Lanes seen fewer than 5 times
    keep the dealer's number. Used everywhere: prices, gig fees, pairs
    Why: The typical fee carries about 13% random noise that drivers ignore (booked fee/typical =
    1.10 when the noise is -15%, 0.88 at +15%)
    Evidence: +195 ± 128 a week on 48 untouched weeks (h-), +403 ± 119 on 48 screening weeks (m-);
    gain is on the fee side

 2. Read the dealer's note (request)
    Finds "not ready before" (car ready later) and "must arrive by" (earlier deadline) in Finnish,
    Swedish and English, typos and word hours included
    Why: Notes that move the ready time or the deadline made 29 to 34% of such orders late
    Evidence: 612 of 619 ready-later notes read to the minute, 327 of 328 earlier deadlines; +308 ±
    92 a week over 65 weeks (+94 ± 137 on clean weeks)

 3. Move the deadline with a late ready day (request)
    A car ready a day or more later moves its deadline by the same business days (weekends do not
    count)
    Why: The rules: a car ready a day later has its deadline moved by a day
    Evidence: Rules

 4. Enough time for the drive (request) [minSlackH = 3]
    An option is sold only if its deadline (or the note's) leaves 3 hours beyond the ready time
    plus the drive
    Why: Less slack leaves no time to find a driver
    Evidence: kit-03 default; tuner o3/o4: 4 hours no better

 5. Last pickup not passed (request)
    Drops an option whose last pickup time (deadline minus drive) is already past
    Why: It cannot be on time
    Evidence: Rules

 6. Plan the driver fee per option (request) [lateCost, openMin, openMax, raiseMax, steps = 4, 0.706, 1.6, 0.2, 4]
    For each option: try every opening fee (0.6 to 1.6 x typical, step 0.05) and hourly raise (0,
    0.05, 0.1, 0.2; 5 raises), walk hour by hour to the last pickup time with the booking model,
    keep the lowest expected cost (fee paid + 4 x typical if no driver). Gives the expected fee and
    the chance a driver books in time
    Why: The fee at which a driver takes the car in time drives every decision
    Evidence: Booking model: unseen-week log loss 0.287 vs 0.455 constant; scaling all planned fees
    by 0.95/1.05/1.10 gives +48 / -286 / -716 a week: the plan is calibrated

 7. Sell only bookable options (request) [minBookChance = 0.95]
    An option is sold at the normal price only if a driver books before its last pickup time with
    95% chance
    Why: Lates cost refunds, dealer trust and the 90% on-time rule
    Evidence: kit-07: 0.90 loses ranked weeks; tuner: 0.93 / 0.97 no better

 8. Hard options are priced up not dropped (request) [hardChance, hardPrice = 0.82, 0.2]
    An option with a booking chance between 0.85 and 0.95 is still sold, 0.2 x list dearer
    Why: A dearer price pays for the risk; wins about 25 more orders a week
    Evidence: kit-10/kit-cmb, part of a bundle +597 ± 129 a week on 48 weeks; tuner o4: 0.1 / 0.3
    no better

 9. Town at capacity is priced up (request) [capPerDriver, fullPrice = 3, 0.25]
    A pickup town is full when our undelivered orders from it reach 3 x the drivers living within
    30 km. Then the whole request is priced 0.25 x list higher instead of declined
    Why: Too many of our cars in one town compete for the same few drivers
    Evidence: Volume set K5e +1,819 ± 170 (kit-ideas); full town priced not declined: part of
    +1,953 ± 174 (kit-09)

10. Deadline day at capacity is priced up (request) [dueCapPer, fullPrice = 0.1, 0.25]
    A deadline day (Monday to Friday) is full when our orders due that day reach 0.10 x the week's
    drivers. An option due on a full day is priced 0.25 x list higher
    Why: Friday-due orders held 40% of all lates; this cap kept weeks ranked
    Evidence: Cap at 0.09: 32/32 ranked vs 30/32 (kit-ideas); 0.10 found by tuner o3, +194 ± 71 a
    week on h- (46/48 ranked vs 47/48)

11. Price choice (price) [markupMin, markupMax = 1.28, 2]
    One price multiple for the request, searched from 1.28 to 2.0 x list in steps of 0.01 (both
    moved by the shifts below): the one with the highest chance to win x (price - expected fee -
    capacity cost). The same multiple goes to every option sold
    Why: The competitor is 26 to 54% above list, different each week; win odds fall about 10% per
    1% of price
    Evidence: Floor 1.28 with capacity cost (row 12): +601 ± 165 a week on h- vs floor 1.38; tuner
    o4: 1.24 / 1.32 both worse

12. Capacity cost (price) [capLam, capConst = 4, 1]
    Subtracts 4 EUR x (drive hours + 1) from the margin in the price choice, so a car that holds a
    driver longer needs a higher price
    Why: Drivers are the binding constraint at level 8: 43% of quotes sat on the old floor; only 20
    to 36% of drivers ever book us
    Evidence: +601 ± 165 a week on h- (with floor 1.28), 47/48 ranked vs 45/48; capLam 3 / 5 / 6 /
    8 all worse; net per driver-hour now flat 10 to 14 EUR in every segment

13. Round trip discount (price) [pairBack = 0.05]
    A request whose pickup is within 30 km of an open gig's delivery and whose delivery is within
    30 km of that gig's pickup is priced 0.05 x list lower
    Why: It completes a round trip: the driver has no trip home to pay
    Evidence: Part of +1,953 ± 174 a week (kit-09 pairs bundle); 0.10 no better (+34 ± 68)

14. Competitor outage (price) [outage, outageN, outageGap = 0.3, 6, 0.3]
    Between 08:00 and 18:00: when at least 6 of today's answers within 80 km of the pickup beat the
    win model by 0.3 on average, price 0.3 x list higher there
    Why: Once a week the competitor is down in one region for a working day; there we win 62% even
    at 1.70 to 1.85 x list
    Evidence: +465 ± 135 on first 16 weeks, +156 ± 104 over 32 (kit-ideas); 0.45 / 0.5 no better

15. Profit floor (price) [minProfit = 0.1]
    An option's price must be at least 1.1 x its expected fee, or it is left out
    Why: Never sell below cost
    Evidence: kit-03 default

16. Decline (price)
    When nothing is left the request is answered with 10 x list (a quote that loses). Not answering
    would cost dealer goodwill
    Why: Rules: a request not quoted lowers the dealer's willingness; a losing quote does not
    Evidence: Rules

17. This week's price level (learning) [worldN = 300]
    After every answer (won or lost), refits one number, the week's competitor level, on our last
    300 answers (4 Newton steps, prior = how much weeks differ, from model.json). quote() uses it
    in the win model
    Why: The competitor's level differs every week by about ±0.59 in log odds
    Evidence: Window 150 / 600: no better (96-week check: -255 / +126)

18. Dealer trust (learning)
    Counts each dealer's on-time orders (cap 25), halved by a late order. The win model reads it:
    each point raises the price the dealer accepts by about 1%
    Why: Rules: level 8 dealers accept 1% more per on-time order
    Evidence: D01 trust averages 14.5 of 25 at its requests (0.7 on Monday; 22 by Thursday)

19. Planned schedule (fees) [lateCost, openMin, openMax, raiseMax, steps = 4, 0.706, 1.6, 0.2, 4]
    A new gig opens at the planned fee and rises hourly by the planned raise (row 6)
    Why: Drivers book when the fee pays them; the plan finds the cheapest path to a booking in time
    Evidence: Flat fees (no raises) lose at every level tried: 0.85 to 1.15 x typical, -1,364 to
    -4,389 a week

20. Early ramp (fees) [rampTop, rampWindowH = 1.45, 10]
    A gig still open 10 hours before its last pickup time climbs hourly from its last fee to 1.6 x
    typical, ending 3 hours before the last pickup time
    Why: Raise early, before the deadline warning, rather than pay the urgent fee
    Evidence: +820 a week (kit-05); tuner: 1.4 / 1.8 and windows 7 / 13 no better

21. Urgent fee after a deadline warning (fees) [steepOpen, steepStep = 1.3, 0.15]
    When the world warns (3 hours before the last pickup time, no driver yet): 1.3 x typical, +0.15
    every half hour, 4 times. A two-car gig is taken apart first
    Why: After the warning only a higher fee finds a driver
    Evidence: K5b (with row 22): about +1,100 a week over the defaults 2.0 / 0.25 (kit-05,
    kit-ideas)

22. Late anyway (fees) [lateFee = 0.832]
    Once the last pickup time has passed with no driver, the fee goes back to 1.2 x typical, then
    +0.2 every 6 hours up to 2
    Why: The car is late whatever we pay; the urgent fee buys nothing more
    Evidence: Part of K5b (row 21); tuner: 1.0 / 1.4 no better

23. Note deadline warning (fees)
    When the note's deadline is earlier than the option's, the urgent fee (row 21) starts 3 hours
    before the note's last pickup time; the world's own warning would come too late
    Why: The world's warning counts the option's deadline
    Evidence: Part of the note reader (row 2)

24. Two cars one driver (pairs) [pairKm, pairSlackH = 40, 6]
    A new one-car order joins an open, unbooked one-car gig of ours when the first car's delivery
    is within 30 km of the second car's pickup, 6 hours are to spare and the two need at most 12
    hours of driving. Not for cars with plates, manual licence, urgent or note-changed orders
    Why: One driver pays one trip to the first pickup and one trip home: two cars book at about
    0.80 x the sum of their typical fees
    Evidence: +1,953 ± 174 a week on 32 new weeks (kit-09 bundle); pairKm 45 / pairSlackH 4 no
    better

25. Pair fee (pairs) [pairCap, pairTop = 0.9, 1.3]
    A pair's planned schedule is capped at 0.9 x the sum of the typical fees, and its early ramp
    climbs to 1.3
    Why: A pair must be cheaper than two single gigs
    Evidence: Tuner: 0.85 / 0.95 and pairTop steps no better

26. Split an unbooked pair (pairs) [pairHoldH = 3]
    A two-car gig nobody booked after 4 hours is taken apart; each car goes alone
    Why: Pairs never booked and split only at the warning were 33% late
    Evidence: +1,083 ± 243 a week vs no split (kit-09); 2.5 / 5.5 worse

27. Premium cars (posting)
    A premium car's gig is open to experienced drivers only
    Why: Rules: fine 150 EUR a car otherwise
    Evidence: Rules

28. Plates and manual gearbox from the note (posting)
    "No plates" in the note: the pickup asks for transfer plates. "Manual": only drivers with a
    manual licence
    Why: Otherwise the first visit fails (plates or licence) and costs half the fee
    Evidence: Found 100% of 220 true cases, no false alarm

29. Charging stop (posting)
    An electric car under 30% charge gets a charging stop
    Why: Rules: 90 minutes late and a 100 EUR fine otherwise
    Evidence: Rules

30. Retry a failed visit (posting)
    Not ready or closed: post again from 2 hours later (or the ready time plus the deadline delay).
    No plates: bring plates. Licence: manual drivers only
    Why: The car must still be moved
    Evidence: Kit starter

31. Repost an expired gig (posting)
    A gig whose window ended before a driver booked it is posted again without its own windows
    Why: A late car can still be collected and delivered
    Evidence: Kit starter

32. Changed order (posting)
    An order the dealer sends elsewhere is withdrawn and posted again (unless a driver holds it)
    Why: The world sends the car to the new place
    Evidence: Kit starter

33. Trade-in over 12 hours (posting)
    A trade-in whose two legs need more than 12 hours goes as one gig per car
    Why: Rules: a multi-car gig may plan at most 12 hours
    Evidence: Rules

34. Booking model (model)
    Chance a driver books in the next hour: logistic on fee / typical (with bends at 1.0 and 1.4),
    drivers near pickup and delivery, drive hours, private address, premium, our load in the town,
    typical fee, time of day, weekday, how long the gig is open, hours left, hours until ready,
    urgent
    Why: Fitted on 160 weeks of random fees (kit-03 explore)
    Evidence: Unseen weeks: log loss 0.287 vs 0.455 for a constant guess

35. Win model (model)
    Chance the dealer buys at a price: logistic on ln(price / list) (weight -10.1), a weight per
    pickup and delivery town, drive hours, trade-in, private delivery, same-day offered, number of
    options, dealer trust (+0.10 a point), dealer lates (-0.10)
    Why: Fitted on 1.0 million quotes of 1,561 weeks
    Evidence: Unseen weeks: log loss 0.506 vs 0.633; weeks differ by 0.59 (learned live, row 17)

WHAT DID NOT WORK
-----------------
Requote before the dealer decides; flat fees; delayed posting; direct offers (early, chain,
rescue); buying dealer trust; learning the competitor beyond the week level (day, dealer, place,
lane shape); competitor lane tables; refitting the booking model on our own weeks; per-lane booking
offsets; driver counts weighted by completed gigs; per-option prices; same-day premium; capacity
cost scaled by town, day, load, roster or weekday. Details: research/LEDGER.md.
